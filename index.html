How I work

The interesting problem in almost everything I build is the same one: something in the system is powerful and unreliable, and my job is to decide exactly what it is allowed to touch.

A language model is very good at reading a failed payment and very bad at being trusted with money. So in the recovery engine it picks one of five fixed actions and twelve deterministic rules decide the rest — and I could then prove the whole thing was worth ₹11.71 lakh more per ₹1 crore of failed volume than the baseline, because the decision boundary was small enough to measure.

The same shape keeps showing up. In the video editor the model chooses which clips matter and never touches a frame number, so every cut can explain itself with a rule id. In the face-match ledger the chain doesn’t take my word for the similarity score — it recomputes it. In the RAG benchmark every published number is behind one make target, because a number nobody can re-derive isn’t a result.

I’m in my third year at MVGR in Vizag, writing C++ daily for data structures and algorithms, and building the rest of this in the evenings. I’m looking for internships where correctness is something the team argues about.
